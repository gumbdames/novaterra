# NOVATERRA Fun Audit — 2026-10-02

> RESEARCH + PLAN ONLY. No game code was written or changed for this audit.
> Five researchers (game designer, game-feel/UX, systems analyst reading the
> actual code, pacing/tension analyst, ideation specialist) worked
> independently from `docs/GAME_MECHANICS.md`, `docs/HOW_TO_PLAY.md`,
> `docs/research/game-design.md`, the campaign missions, and `game/src`
> (`sim/`, `ui/`, `render/`, `audio/`). This document synthesizes their
> findings into one prioritized plan.
>
> North Star, in the user's stated order: **no bugs, flowing game, great game
> mechanics, fun to play.** Everything below is ordered by fun-per-risk,
> correctness first. The user verifies on their own machine; this document
> says what's actually unfun, not what's polite.

## 1. How the game actually plays today (the honest version)

### 1.1 The core loop

Survey → zone → connect utilities → unpause → watch → read the city back
through overlays and the advisor → fix the worst problem → grow, age up,
repeat. The city earns eight resources on real production chains;
population converts to manpower; ages unlock through permanent either/or
National Program choices; when you want war (or the AI brings it), the same
economy that feeds your people feeds your army — fuel, ammo, and materials
move through physical depots, trucks, tankers, and load/unload orders. You
win by destroying the enemy's war machine, out-earning them, out-housing
them, or finishing a Monument — or you skip war entirely and build
endlessly in peaceful mode. The genre promise: **"Build the nation. Defend
the future"** — one game that is both a real city-builder and a real RTS,
where the city is the supply base and war is optional.

### 1.2 The 30-minute skirmish, minute by minute

Setup: 4,000 funds, 1,500 materials, 20 manpower, 2 engineers, 4 rifles,
zero buildings. Tick is 30 Hz; pause/1×/2×/4×.

- **0:00–2:00 — the only dense decision window.** ~6 buildings placed (power
  plant 900f/350m, 60s build; water pump; farm 300/80, 15s; 3–4 houses
  120/40, 10s; barracks 700/250, 40s; lab 650/220, 45s), zones painted,
  power lines/pipes dragged. Every meaningful opening decision fits in
  ~15 clicks.
- **2:00–6:00 — boring stretch #1: watching concrete dry.** Construction is
  pure time. Income is literally zero until the power plant finishes
  (~1:00–1:30) and utilities connect. A new player sits at 1× watching
  progress bars; a veteran hits 4×. Nothing to decide here.
- **6:00–12:00 — boring stretch #2: the trickle.** Buildings come online.
  Income is tiny (4 houses ≈ 0.4 funds/s in tax); first research upgrade at
  ~7–8 min if the lab was rushed; manpower trickles. Units train
  **instantly** — click palette, click map, unit exists. Lower difficulties:
  nothing threatens you. You see the whole map (no fog of war), so scouting
  is free.
- **12:00–20:00 — the actual game.** The good part, and the shortest
  relative to the buildup: army composition and counters (the counter table
  is genuinely deep), first age advance, a second district, maybe a
  ceasefire to rebuild. Real decisions, real tension — roughly 8 minutes.
- **20:00–30:00 — boring stretch #3: the cleanup.** The AI owns no physical
  buildings (all virtual, no footprint). Conquest = "rival has no units and
  no buildings," so there is no base to destroy, no production to cut off.
  The AI trains 1 unit per think, spawning from thin air at its map corner —
  which you can see, because there's no fog of war. The endgame is
  spawn-camping an indestructible spawner until the victory check happens to
  catch zero alive. Whack-a-mole, not a climax.

**Decision-to-waiting ratio:** ~30 discrete player decisions in the first
12 minutes against ~9 minutes of pure waiting at 1×. The ratio flips good
in minutes 12–20, then degrades into cleanup. The alternate victories
don't fix pacing: economic needs 100,000 funds (25× starting treasury —
a 30+ minute grind), population needs 10,000 housed, monument needs
Information age + 3,000f/1,200m + 90s build while defending it.

### 1.3 The campaign arc ("The First Term")

Escalation skeleton is sound; flesh is uneven. AI ramp: none → cadet →
citizen → citizen → commander → general → general → marshal. Scripted raids
escalate properly and are telegraphed ("Northern border breached!").
Mission verdicts: M1 is infrastructure (good tutorial, no stakes); M2 is a
breather, slightly flat; M3 is the campaign's first real heartbeat; M4's
peaceful path (stockpile 2,500 goods) lets you skip the naval fantasy that
is the mission's whole identity; M5 is conceptually strong (an age *race*)
but you can't see the rival's progress and races need a visible scoreboard;
M6 is a proper mid-campaign peak; **M7 is the campaign's slump** — the
peaceful path (2 media centers + 400 influence) is a pure waiting game,
the military path (destroy 30 units) is a grind quota; M8 earns its
finale. The two endings are weak stakes: scoring is invisible during play
(+2 diplomat for zero kills, +1 for zero losses; +2 commander for 30+
kills), a mixed player ties into Peacemaker by default, and the endings
change only the final debrief screen — M8 plays identically regardless.
Cross-mission consequences were designed (the design doc's draft: M3's
choice matters in M5) and not shipped.

### 1.4 What the player actually does (verb audit, from code)

- **Core:** placeBuilding, paintZone, buildPowerLine/buildPipe, moveUnit,
  moveGroup, stopUnit, attackUnit, attackBuilding, spawnUnit (instant —
  the single most pacing-distorting fact in the game), researchUpgrade.
- **Situational:** buildRoad/buildRail (roads are now optional per the
  2026-09-30 directive — decoration with ×0.5 move cost), setTaxRate,
  setPolicy, setSpecialization, demolish, superweapons (6000f/2500m/150s
  build + 10-min cooldown — trophy content by the time you can afford
  them), logistics orders (the system runs itself), diplomacy verbs,
  intel verbs (buried 3 clicks deep behind intelHQ + Information age).
- **Dead or near-dead:** establishTradeRoute (pay 500 funds, get 3/s
  forever — an income button, no moving parts), the engineer unit (no
  mechanic: no construction, no repair — a 50-fund civilian that deals
  5 damage; the tutorial's first lesson teaches you to bond with a unit
  that does nothing), the influence stockpile (spent only on ages 3+:
  100/250/500 — a single toll booth, not a decision axis).
- **Notable absences:** no production queues, no rally points (deliberately
  skipped per B4), no patrol/attack-move, no building repair, no fog of
  war for the player (the human gets wallhacks; the AI is sight-gated —
  this kills the recon roster's purpose).

### 1.5 Automation audit (what plays itself)

- **Good automation:** organic growth (paint zones → buildings auto-place
  every 10s — the city-builder's engine, and it hums); logistics AI
  (supply ships auto-find the neediest friendly — fuel/ammo would be
  unplayable micro without this; the remaining decisions, depot placement
  and fleet composition, are the right ones).
- **Bad automation:** the mayor auto-sets all four tax rates *every second*
  and auto-builds buildings on a spiral scan. This doesn't remove tedium —
  it removes the game. The optimal opening is "paint zones, appoint mayor,
  watch." Combined with the general (auto-attacks/defends with assigned
  units), a player can delegate *both halves* and be a pure spectator.
- Minutes 2–12 with mayor + zones is the rational play and it is
  spectating. The highest-APM opening (manual placement of everything) is
  strictly worse than paint-and-delegate. The city-builder half is on
  autopilot; the RTS half has no production friction (instant training).
  The game feels like an RTS wearing a city-builder costume rather than a
  hybrid — the one thing that can't be delegated is army tactics in
  minutes 12–20.

## 2. Genre research: what makes these games fun, and where NOVATERRA stands

Drawn from the design literature and the postmortems in
`docs/research/game-design.md` Part A. Proven patterns first, hype
separated from fact.

| # | Pattern (proven) | Source | Kept? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Counter triangles — every unit has something it beats and something that beats it | *StarCraft*, *RA2*; the design doc cites RA2's roster discipline ("every unit serves a unique purpose, no strategy without a counter") | **Kept.** vsLight/vsMedium/vsHeavy/vsAir multipliers are real; the Commander+ AI builds the counter table. "Scout, then build the counter" works. |
| 2 | Age-up as commitment — pay now, fight later | *Age of Empires 2* (Feudal ~10 min, Castle ~16 min danger windows) | **Kept — and improved.** Per-side ages with no free-riding; the age race is real. But the *danger windows* aren't: the AI ages too slowly and invisibly (§4). |
| 3 | Fog + pause = dread you can plan around | *They Are Billions* (fog + sound cues + the pause key; the reveal *is* the gameplay) | **Broken.** No fog of war for the player. Terrain fully visible; only enemy units sight-gated on the minimap. The entire intel roster (Recon Team, drones, recon UAVs, AWACS, listening posts) and half the spy fantasy have no darkness to operate in. |
| 4 | Fixed, escalating, telegraphed, un-negotiable wave schedule as the heartbeat | *They Are Billions* final-wave structure; the L4D AI Director (A5 research) | **Broken in skirmish.** The AI is purely reactive — attacks only *visible* enemies; no scheduled offensives exist in code. Soak: a "pure cold war, 0 kills" game was recorded. Campaign raids are the closest analogue but they're one-shots, not a schedule. |
| 5 | Superweapons-as-clocks that end stalemates | *RA2* superweapon pacing (Eurogamer retrospective, A2 research) | **Broken.** Aegis/Storm live at Ascendance (25,000f + 10,000m + 500 influence) — unreachable in practice; AI marshal stalls at Connectivity, commanders/generals at Foundation. The pacer is priced out of the game. |
| 6 | AI decision-quality ladder with small disclosed handicaps | *Zero Hour Reforged* (A5 research) | **Kept.** The five tiers are honestly differentiated; the AI never cheats (same sight, same prices, same orders). Best practice, rare, credit given. |
| 7 | Every production step a ratio puzzle you designed | *Factorio* (Dan Cook's "skill atom": the fun is the player-authored solution) | **Broken.** Once zoned and taxed, the city runs itself. Roads carry no traffic, pipes/lines are boolean, citizens are decorative. Demand-driven growth and desirability with visible causes (pollution, shoreline, amenities) are genuinely good — but placement is one decision, made once. |
| 8 | Positive asymmetry — full shared tree, every faction does 2–3 things *better* | *Age of Empires 2* (the design doc itself cites this as the model to steal) | **Broken.** Both sides train from the same 97-unit roster with identical stats. The design doc's Republic-vs-Kestrel *doctrine* (C11) never shipped. Every matchup is the same composition puzzle. |
| 9 | The pausable RTS | *They Are Billions* (space-pause + speed control) | **Kept.** Space-pause + 1×/2×/4×; sessions designed for hours across days. |
| 10 | Every currency a decision axis; inflation is a bug | The design doc's own A3 rule | **Half-broken.** Funds/materials/fuel genuinely cross systems (fuel burns in vehicles *and* plants *and* factories — the best-interplay resource in the game). But manpower is army-only, food is population-only, research is upgrades-only, goods are funds-only, influence is ages-only (one toll booth), and the market is a fixed ±20% panic button, not a market. Five single-channel tokens, not decision axes. |
| 11 | War-weariness / no mopping farmhouses | Most commercial RTS (*SC2*, *AoE2*) never solved this | **Kept — rare idea.** Defeat fires when your army is gone and no military building stands, even with houses intact. Small, correct, unusual. |
| 12 | Physical supply — fuel and ammo as things that move | *Company of Heroes* abstracted supply to auras; everything else to upkeep | **Kept — the best system in the game.** Physical fuel/ammo/materials through depots, trucks, tankers, ammo ships, load/unload orders, emergency refuel as a decision rather than a death sentence, nuclear units as the deliberate exception. "The Naval Shipyard is dry; the Naval Base is the fleet's shipping interface" is genuinely elegant rules writing. Fusing that with a city-builder economy where the same fuel powers the grid is a real design idea. It needs UI/tutoring love — highest learning-cost system per unit of player-facing drama — but the design is sound and distinctive. |

### Patterns correctly rejected (hype, not proven)

- **Ability bloat.** Rightly avoided (the design doc cites RA3's ability-bloat trap) — but the game landed at *zero* expressive micro. Combat is won in the build menu, not on the field. That's an acceptable identity for a builder-leaning game; it just shouldn't pretend otherwise.
- **Full traffic simulation.** An L with a real perf cost against the 60fps-on-mid-range-laptop budget. The boolean→quantitative step (§5, item D2) gets 80% of the puzzle for 20% of the cost.
- **Real-time multiplayer-adjacent systems** (neutral actors, third-party targeting) — see the Vostok Combine raid discussion in §4 and §6.

## 3. Gap analysis: what's actually unfun, specific to NOVATERRA

### 3.1 The boring stretches (pacing)

1. **2:00–6:00 — watching concrete dry.** Zero income until the power plant finishes and utilities connect. Nothing to decide.
2. **6:00–12:00 — the trickle.** Tiny income, first upgrade at ~8 min, nothing threatening on low difficulties, instant training removes all production drama.
3. **15:00–22:00 — the slump.** The air goes out; five causes stack: the AI cannot finish games (soak: 7 AI-vs-AI games, **none reached a natural conclusion** — "the loser keeps retraining from virtual buildings and the winner never presses the attack"); no scheduled pressure (purely reactive AI — 0-kill cold wars recorded); the age ladder prices out the mid-game (AI never reaches Industry — it has zero influence income; the "army now vs. tech later" tension can't bite if "later" is an hour away); the superweapon clock never fires; the wonder countdown was dropped in implementation (`session.ts:305` is "first to complete a Monument," not the documented "complete and survive the 10-minute countdown").
4. **20:00–30:00 — the cleanup.** Spawn-camping an indestructible spawner. Whack-a-mole, not a climax.

Net: a healthy 15-minute game wearing a 30-minute costume. Act 1 and the Act 2 opening work; there is no midpoint reversal, no "all is lost" beat, and Act 3 arrives only when the player manually ends it.

### 3.2 The feel gaps (first-60-seconds test and feedback)

1. **The game opens on empty terrain.** Camera spawns at map center; your 6 starting units are 255 world units away at HUMAN_CORNER, off-screen. No intro pan, no "here are your people." First 10 seconds read as "broken or empty." Trivially fixable, maximally first-impression-damaging.
2. **No box-select.** The `handleDragRect` fallback in `game.ts:2975` is **dead code** — the select-rect only exists while a placement tool is armed. Managing a 20-tank army = 20 shift-clicks. Largest mechanical friction in the game. (Fixing it means re-litigating the grab-pan gesture contract — deliberately *not* recommended; see §6.)
3. **Off-screen attacks have no location cue.** The game plays a throttled (30s), non-positional `underAttack` alarm, and a code comment claims "the selection ping carries the location" — **no such ping exists anywhere in render or game code.** Your base can be raided off-screen with no minimap flash, no edge indicator, no camera-jump. Worst missing combat feedback in the product.
4. **The advisor doesn't name things.** Muse's `buildingComplete`/`unitTrained`/`unitLost` events carry the literal strings `'building'` / `'unit'`; the persona lines were authored for names (`{event.unit}` interpolation exists) but the controller never passes them. "… — building." / "… — unit." is what the player hears.
5. **Anonymous completions.** Research finishes as a chime with no name — open Management → Research to find out what. Trained units appear silently off-screen with a double-beep. Veterancy promotions (Recruit → Elite) pass with no audio, no toast, no flash — the game's purest positive-feedback moment, unmarked.
6. **Age toast lies.** `strings.ts:235` hardcodes "Age advanced: Connectivity." for *every* age advance — contradicting Muse's persona line, which names the right age.
7. **Race victories are invisible races.** The victory picker added economic/population/monument races *without race telemetry*: the rival can approach 100,000 funds or finish a Monument and the player has zero visibility — no rival treasury line, no rival population line. "Players can't feel a race they can't see."
8. **End screens have no statistics.** Title + detail + art + two buttons. Muse already tracks lifetime kills/losses; they're never surfaced. Winning a 45-minute war reports the same payload as winning a 5-minute one. (C6 art/drift is in flight — art isn't the gap; the scorecard is.)
9. **Enemy sabotage has no alarm.** Your own intel ops get a chime; *enemy* sabotage landing on your buildings gets no SFX at all. A building quietly going offline for 45 seconds feels like the game cheating when you finally notice.
10. **Threat meter sees through fog.** `muse/director.ts:computeThreat` sums *all* enemy military value regardless of visibility. The minimap "never maphacks" but the threat meter leaks total enemy army strength to a player who hasn't scouted a single unit. Free intel wearing a UI skin.

### 3.3 The dead systems

1. **The engineer** — no construction, no repair, no capture. The tutorial's first lesson teaches you to bond with a unit that does nothing.
2. **Land trade routes** — 500 funds in, 3 funds/sec out, forever. An income button with no trucks, no endpoints, no gameplay.
3. **The recon roster vs. wallhacks** — Recon Team, recon UAV, recon plane, drones exist for a fog-of-war game you're not playing. Only stealth/detection survives.
4. **Spies as pressure** — Commander+ is supposed to infiltrate and sabotage. Soak: **0 spy missions in 14 owner-games.** The AI builds listening posts but never trains a spy. This pressure source exists in docs only.
5. **Superweapons for the player** — trophy content; you've usually won by the time you can afford them.
6. **Rail** — real but fiddly: cell-by-cell laying for transit earnings that never justify the micro vs. another factory.

### 3.4 The onboarding cliff

M1 teaches **controls** (select, move, build, zone, drag lines) well, with triggers tied to player actions. It does not teach **verbs**, and nothing else does:

1. **"A placed building is not a working building."** The first unconnected district silently runs at 25% output; the tutorial teaches *how* to drag a power line but never *why the first district underperforms*. A first-time skirmish player builds a district, watches it sit at quarter output, and concludes the game is broken. (B14 root-causing diagnoses after the fact — diagnosis-after-failure is not teaching.)
2. **Right-click is three verbs** (move / attack / siege) and the mental model is taught nowhere except HOW_TO_PLAY.md.
3. **The economy has no mental model.** Eight resources with good *display* (B10 net-rate chips) but no teaching: "funds are the constraint, materials gate construction, manpower gates armies, food gates growth."

### 3.5 Victory and defeat moments

- **No run-up.** Conquest ends the instant the last building falls — no "their capital is burning" beat, no slow-down. Earned victories need a climax, not a tripwire.
- **Race victories end mid-thought** — they trip silently at a threshold while you might be mid-battle. The dropped wonder countdown is the specific loss.
- **Defeat edge case:** the war-weariness shortcut fires while your civilian city still stands — from the player's chair, "my city is fine, why did I lose?" Defensible rule; it needs an explicit "your war core has fallen" warning *before* the screen, not just the screen after.
- **Losing doesn't teach.** No post-defeat readout of what went wrong — when the threat meter flipped, what killed your army, what you never built. And per the soak data, the AI effectively *can't* beat a competent player by annihilation — a defeat the AI can't deliver is a tutorial that can't teach.

### 3.6 The identity gaps (design-level)

1. **No strategic identity.** One roster vs. itself; no matchup knowledge to learn; no "who am I / who are they." In Schell's terms (*The Art of Game Design*), the interesting choices are all tactical; the strategic choice of *who to be* doesn't exist.
2. **The intel game has no darkness** (§2, pattern 3). A full intel economy — three assets, spies, infiltration, sabotage, detection radii — on a fully lit map.
3. **The city has no optimization puzzle** (§2, pattern 7). The game's own research (A1) names road-building and emergent traffic a *core pleasure* of the genre, then shipped roads as optional decoration.
4. **The spectating problem.** The rational play (paint zones, appoint mayor, appoint general) is to watch the game play itself for the first 12 minutes. The game rewards spectating its own economy.

## 4. Novel ideas (bold but fitting, each with its steelman case against it)

Judged against the North Star in order: no bugs first, then flow, mechanics, fun. All are deterministic by construction (seeded RNG streams, tick-based deadlines, command-queue resolution) and build *after* the in-flight determinism hardening lands.

### Idea 1 — "The Envoy at the Gates" (S)

**The fantasy:** You are the President, and diplomacy just walked up your driveway. An enemy convoy — black SUVs, little flags, a white-flag escort — crosses the map to your HQ, parks outside, and waits. Muse: *"The Directorate has sent an envoy. They'll wait sixty seconds."* Accept a ceasefire and doves rise over your capital (the flock system exists); refuse and the convoy peels out. Diplomacy stops being three menu buttons and becomes a scene you remember.

**Mechanic:** one new sim unit kind with a `diplomaticImmunity: true` flag; `combat.ts` skips immune units in opportunistic acquisition (one-line gate, mirroring the existing ceasefire gate). Triggers: (a) AI accepts your ceasefire — the 5-minute clock starts *when the envoy reaches your HQ* instead of an instant state flip; (b) tribute ≥ 2,000 funds while at war can summon an envoy carrying a ceasefire offer if seeded disposition clears a threshold — player-reachable on demand; (c) a proud AI's *refusal* is delivered in person, with Muse warning the front is about to heat up. Sim state `world.diplomacy.envoy = { state: 'inbound'|'waiting'|'departing', offer, deadlineTick }`; spawn at map-edge land cell nearest player HQ (seeded jitter); deadline = tick + 1,800 (60 s); `answerEnvoy(accept|decline)` through the command queue with loud validation. Timeout → seeded default (ceasefire still starts — no punishment for slow players). Ceremony events drain through the same pattern as `world.combatEvents`.

**Size:** S. One unit kind + flag, one combat gate, a small diplomacy state machine, UI card, Muse lines. Zero new art (civilian vehicle models + flag decals).

**Steelman case against:** It's ceremony around a system most RTS players use twice per campaign — fiddly polish that could have gone into combat feel. It risks training players that diplomacy = a 60-second interruption, making them *less* likely to use it. And if the envoy's arrival ever desyncs from the ceasefire state (player destroys the AI mid-arrival), the moment curdles into a bug report — the state machine needs an explicit "war ended, recall envoy" path, which is more code than the feature looks. Verdict: build player-side only; never cut the arrival ceremony (without the convoy, the doves, and the 60-second wait, it's just a dialog box).

### Idea 2 — "Luminaries" (M)

**The fantasy:** Once per age, someone remarkable walks into your capital and asks for an audience: a defecting Kestrel scientist, a logistics prodigy, a war hero, a tycoon with a suspicious charter, a whistleblower with a folder. A real unit with a gold ring, and a presidential call with costs on *both* sides. Months later you remember "the age I turned the spy" the way Civ players remember their great people.

**Mechanic:** new leaf module `sim/luminaries.ts` (like `seaTrade.ts` — no import cycles): a **data deck** of event cards (ship 6 in 0.1 Alpha; writers add cards without touching sim logic). Trigger: hooked into `ages.ts` — on age advance, draw from the `rng` `events` stream, filter by pure `eligibility` functions ("at war," "has a navy," "owns a Veteran+ unit," "treasury > X"); if none eligible, no luminary that age — scarcity is a feature. The luminary spawns as a non-combat civilian at the capital; `pendingDecision { cardId, unitId, deadlineTick }` in plain snapshot-covered world data (3 min = 5,400 ticks); `resolveLuminary(choiceId)`; deadline applies the seeded default. Three sketched cards: **The Defector** (turn them: +200 surveillance assets and enemy production marked 90 s / interrogate: +150 research / public trial: +100 influence, −10 disposition); **The War Hero** (retire a Veteran+ unit to a drill instructor with a +50% XP aura — and it gets a *name*, seeded from a name list / keep in the field: +50 influence); **The Whistleblower** (transparency: −500 funds, +150 influence / cover-up: free now, but if counter-intel > 100 it stays buried else it leaks in 5 min, −200 influence — this one *teaches* the intel stockpile's value).

**Size:** M. Sim side is small (queue, spawner, resolver); the bulk is card data + writing + the UI card. That's a virtue: content scales independently of engineering.

**Steelman case against:** It's Frostpunk/Civ homework — not novel, and novelty was the brief. It risks the "advisor spam" failure mode the design doc explicitly warns about (A1: surface the worst problems first). If the writing is merely competent rather than good, luminaries become a tax: a popup you dismiss for +50 influence. Card *eligibility* reads broad game state, so each card needs its own headless test with a constructed world. Verdict: ship 6 cards, not 24; one great card beats six flat ones; this is the drama engine the peaceful endless mode desperately needs — right now peaceful has no victory and no story beats.

### Idea 3 — "The Vostok Combine" (M)

**The fantasy:** War is expensive, and somewhere offshore there's a freighter full of shells that doesn't care about your politics. *"A Combine freighter is holding offshore. Premium prices, no questions."* Top up ammo before the big push — or watch the Kestrel buy too, and realize what *they're* planning. And if you're ruthless, you can sink the freighter and take the cargo… once. They never come back.

**Mechanic:** new `sim/combine.ts`: `world.combine = { nextVisitTick, state: 'away'|'inbound'|'anchored'|'departing', anchoredUntilTick, prices, stock, burned }`. Seeded schedule (first visit ~8 min ±2, then every 12–18 min); 60-s Muse warning + map ping. The freighter (reuses the Cargo Freighter model — zero new art) anchors 3 min; purchases via the Trade panel (`combineBuy`) at **2× market rate with ±15% seeded drift** — insultingly small quantities, offensively high prices, so the Phase-3 logistics chain stays the primary economy and the Combine stays the emergency valve. The Combine also *buys influence* (8 funds each, cap 100/visit) — a funds/influence sink that fights the A3 inflation problem. **The tell:** at commander+, if the AI is war-planning and ammo-starved with funds, it buys — and Muse reports it: *"Kestrel logistics just bought Combine shells. Draw your own conclusions."* The AI's logistics become readable intent (pillar 8: legible AI). Anti-cheese: mirror the 20% market spread so there's no buy-low/sell-high loop.

**Size:** M. One module, one AI rule, one UI tab, a freighter behavior reusing the sea-trade ferry pattern.

**Steelman case against:** This is the most *systemic* idea in a game that already has eight resources, a market, trade routes, and a logistics chain — it risks being the ninth knob nobody turns. The raid is a trap choice in disguise: sinking the freighter is *always* wrong (no cargo recovered, permanent lockout, influence hit), so it's not a real decision, it's a one-time anecdote — expensive engineering for that. And the neutral-targeting gate (a unit the AI must never touch but the player may sink) is the biggest bug surface of the three ideas; North Star #1 is "no bugs." Verdict: **ship raid-less first** — the freighter as a pure merchant — and add the raid only after the targeting gate has proven itself via the Envoy's immunity flag. The envoy's combat gate and the Combine's never-target gate should be **one shared flag** (e.g. `neutralNonCombatant`), implemented in the same change.

**Fun-per-risk order:** Envoy (S, a signature moment for the price of a unit flag) → Luminaries (M, the story engine peaceful mode needs; data-driven) → Combine (M, highest emergent-drama ceiling, highest bug surface).

## 5. The plan — prioritized work items

Ordered by fun-per-risk, **correctness first**. Every item names what
changes, why (linked to a finding), size, risk, and dependencies —
including the in-flight C6 (endscreens), C7 (visual day/night), C1 (AI
forward base), and the queued determinism hardening.

### Tier 0 — Correctness & honesty (do first; all S, low risk)

**A1. Fix the age-advanced toast.** `strings.ts:235` hardcodes "Age
advanced: Connectivity." for every age. Use `fillLoc` with the actual age
name. Why: it contradicts the advisor and is visible in every game.
S / Low / no deps.

**A2. Make Muse name things.** Pass entity display names through
`muse/controller.ts` `buildingComplete`/`unitTrained`/`unitLost` events
instead of the literals `'building'`/`'unit'`; the persona templates
already interpolate `{event.unit}`. Why: "… — building." is the advisor
telling you nothing. S / Low / no deps.

**A3. Name research completions.** Diff `world.upgrades[playerId]` in
`snapshotForAudio` (it already sees the array; the differ only counts)
and toast the upgrade's display name alongside the chime. Same family as
A2. S / Low / no deps.

**A4. War-core warning beat.** Before the war-weariness defeat fires,
raise an explicit warning — "Your war core has fallen" Muse line +
threat-meter flash — *before* the screen, not just the screen after.
Why: today the game can end while your civilian city stands, and the
player's chair reads it as "my city is fine, why did I lose?" S / Low /
no deps.

**A5. Resolve the phantom selection ping.** A code comment claims "the
selection ping carries the location" for off-screen attacks; **no such
ping exists.** Either implement the event-ping system (B5) or delete the
stale comment. Why: a comment that describes a system that doesn't exist
is a correctness lie that will mislead the next engineer. S / folds into
B5.

### Tier 1 — First impressions, climaxes, and the missing beats (highest fun-per-risk)

**B1. Intro camera + skirmish opening beat (S / Low / no deps).**
On `startGame`, set the camera target to the human base (HUMAN_CORNER in
`session.ts`) and hold a 2–3s slow zoom-out from distance ~80 to the
default 150, with Muse's gameStart line and a single opening toast naming
the win condition ("Destroy the enemy base" / "First to 100,000 funds" /
etc. from `world.victoryKind`). Why: the first 10 seconds currently read
as "broken or empty," and skirmish has no objective indicator at all.
Cheapest application of Swink's first-60-seconds rule: show the player
their toy, tell them the game.

**B2. Restore the wonder countdown (S / Low / no deps).** When any side
completes a Monument — or crosses 80% of an economic/population victory
threshold — start a 5-minute global countdown with escalating warnings
("The Monument stands. Five minutes until the world acknowledges
Novaterrra."). The rival gets a last-stand window; the leader must
defend. Why: this is the game's *own documented design* (C6: "complete
the Monument wonder and survive the 10-minute countdown," RA2
superweapon pacing) that was dropped in implementation. It's the cheapest
possible climax: a timer, some warnings, and the existing combat.
A run needs a forced ending to feel like a run.

**B3. End-of-game statistics (S / Low / pairs with in-flight C6).**
`endscreen.ts` already has the layout slot; `MuseController` already
tracks kills/losses; world state has duration (tick), peak population,
buildings raised. A 5-line stat block ("27 enemy units destroyed · 11
lost · 41:23 · peak population 1,204 · age reached"). Why: the game
currently asks for 45 minutes and reports back one sentence — Juul's
"juice as reward for effort." Nearly free: read-only UI work, no sim
changes. Ship alongside the C6 art/drift work.

**B4. Victory-progress HUD + race telemetry (S–M / Low / no deps).**
A small "rival watch" strip: your progress toward the picked victory
(conquest %, funds vs 100k, population vs 10k, monument status) *and*
the rival's as intel-flavored estimates, plus a persistent objective line
("Destroy the enemy base" — and, critically, what that actually means
given §3.1). Why: the victory kind is picked at setup and never shown
again; race victories are invisible races; conquest's actual condition
(kill every unit, then the corner-spawner) is uncommunicated. This one
component fixes the "unclear goals" finding across all victory kinds.

**B5. Off-screen event pings (M / Low-Med / no deps).** A minimal
event-marker system: a pooled pulsing marker primitive in `render/`
(ring + icon sprite, ~40 lines), fed from the audio-poll events the game
already computes (`AudioPollEvents.deaths/destroyed/buildsComplete` all
carry world positions). Under-attack → red ping at the damage centroid;
building complete → soft green ping (friendly only); trained unit →
subtle white ping at the production building. Why: closes the P0
location gap *and* the P2 silent-training gap with one system — the
player currently *hears* something happened but can't *see* where.
Throttle (one ping per event class per few seconds) so late-game wars
don't become fireworks. This also resolves A5.

**B6. Scheduled, escalating, telegraphed AI offensives (M / Med / after
determinism hardening).** The AI runs a visible war schedule independent
of contact — border probes from ~8 min, a genuine offensive at ~15 min,
an all-in at ~25 min if the game is still live. Each phase telegraphed
by Muse ("Marshal Dain is massing armor on your eastern border") and the
threat meter, per pillar 8's legibility rule. Diplomacy/ceasefires can
*delay* a phase, never cancel it. Why: this is the They Are Billions
lesson and the L4D Director lesson from the game's own research (A5): an
external, un-negotiable, telegraphed clock is the single most reliable
tension engine in the genre. It fills the 15–22 min slump and fixes the
"pure cold war, 0 kills" pathology — contact becomes guaranteed.
Dependency: the AI must actually be able to *end* games (see B7) or the
clock is theater.

**B7. The AI must be able to finish — C1 acceptance check + fallback (M /
Med / depends on in-flight C1).** Ensure the in-flight C1 forward base
makes the AI's base **physical, destructible, and required for AI
training** (destroy the war factory → the AI can't train tanks). If C1's
forward base is still virtual, conquest stays broken — this needs an
explicit acceptance check, not an assumption. Fallback if C1 can't:
change `checkSkirmishVictory` (`ui/session.ts:329`) so conquest also
completes on **base-zone supremacy** — hold the AI's base zone
(radius around `ai.baseX/baseZ`) uncontested for N ticks with its field
army destroyed. Why: the soak headline — 7 AI-vs-AI games, none reached
a natural conclusion. B6's clock needs an AI that can deliver a defeat,
or every "oh no, they're coming" moment is a bluff the player will learn
to ignore.

**B8. Age-ups as announced global events (S–M / Low-Med / after
determinism hardening).** Age advancement becomes a global, visible
event — both sides notified ("The Directorate has entered the Industry
Age — Arsenal Program"), Muse line, threat-meter nudge. The 60–90 s
after a rival ages up is a deliberate vulnerability/opportunity beat:
they just sank thousands into tech instead of army (the AoE2 tension,
finally legible), and the game says so. Why: the mid-game's problem
isn't lack of systems, it's lack of *beats*. The most important
strategic decision (army now vs. tech later) currently happens silently.
S-sized fallback: ship just the global announcement + Muse line + nudge;
the visibility alone does half the work.

### Tier 2 — Core mechanic repairs (needs user approval + playtesting)

**C1. Real production model: train queues + train times + rally points
(M / Med / after determinism hardening; coordinate with C1 owner — both
touch sim files).** Add `trainUnit` in `sim/units.ts` next to
`spawnUnit` — payload `{kind, owner, buildingId}`, validate
`requiredBuilding` + hangar capacity as today, push to
`building.trainQueue: Array<{kind, trainTicks}>` (new
snapshot-covered field on `BuildingRecord`). A `runTraining` step in the
economy tick advances the head of each queue by `def.trainSeconds` (new
`UnitDef` field, ~5–20s by tier) and spawns at the building's rally
cell on completion. Keep `spawnUnit` for AI/campaign/demo paths. Why:
`spawnUnit` is instant — the entire military game is "have funds → have
army." This single change creates the missing mid-game (queue
management, production capacity as a strategic resource, raidable
factories), makes the AI's virtual production *feel* fair (it already
pays build times virtually), and finally justifies rally points — the B4
cut gets properly un-cut. It also directly attacks boring stretch #1:
the opening becomes *production management*, not waiting.

**C2. Triage the dead weight — no more zombie systems (S–M / Low / no
deps; can start now).**
- (a) **Engineer:** either give it the construction verb the game is
  missing — `placeBuilding` creates a `constructionSite` that only
  progresses while an engineer is within N cells (also fixes boring
  stretch #1 by making the opening interactive) — or delete the unit
  from `startingForces` and the tutorial and stop pretending.
- (b) **Influence:** it has exactly one spend (ages 3+: 100/250/500).
  Make diplomacy cost influence (`demandTribute`/`proposeCeasefire`
  validate `player.influence >= cost` in `sim/diplomacy.ts`) — monuments
  become diplomatically meaningful overnight, and influence becomes a
  decision axis. If not, say so and delete the stockpile.
- (c) **Land trade routes:** either spawn visible truck convoys on roads
  between commercial districts (raisable, real gameplay) or delete
  `establishTradeRoute` and fold the 3/s into the market.
- (d) **Civilian transport roster:** the air tab alone lists Airliner,
  Jumbo Airliner, Regional Jet, Passenger Heli, Seaplane, Cargo Plane —
  several mechanically identical at different sizes. Keep one Cargo
  Plane, one Airliner, one Freighter as trainable; make the rest
  decorative variants. The 97-unit roster is a marketing number, not a
  mechanics number — it weakens the counter-readability the combat is
  built on. S effort, data consolidation, no sim changes.

**C3. Fog of war for the player (L / High / after determinism hardening;
the biggest single item).** An `explored`-style shroud in `sim/world.ts`
+ sight-limited render. Why: this one change resurrects the entire recon
roster, scouting, radar, and the spy game — the design's full intel
economy finally has darkness to operate in. It's the highest-cost item
in the plan; if it's too big for 0.1 Alpha, say so and instead cut the
recon units' player-facing presence (they're tutorialized nowhere and
useless today). Note the sequencing constraint: don't build concurrently
with determinism hardening (touches the same sight/simulation code
paths). The game-feel P3 (threat meter leaks total enemy strength) rides
with this — gate `computeThreat` on visibility or reframe the label as
an estimate.

> **STATUS 2026-10-02 — SHIPPED as genuine player fog of war** (no
> fallback needed: the sim already had a real disc-based sight model in
> `sim/ai.ts`). `sim/fog.ts`: per-owner explored memory (monotonic,
> snapshotted, not digested), `computeVisibleCells` rasterizing the same
> `getSightDiscs` the AI uses, fog system at 30-tick cadence with first-call
> priming. `render/fog.ts`: height-conforming 64×64 shroud plane (+1 draw
> call), read-only view. Entity/shadow/chevron/damage-number filters,
> minimap shroud, and `computeThreat` gated on visibility (P3).

**C4. Onboarding: teach verbs, not just controls (S–M / Low / no deps).**
M1 teaches *how to operate the machine* but not *what the machine is
for*. Cheapest high-leverage change: add one "fix a disconnected
building" beat to M1 — a pre-placed unpowered house, the advisor flags
it, walk the player through dragging a power line to it. Teach the verb
"connect," not just the control "drag." Follow-ups: a skirmish opening
hint naming right-click's three verbs (move / attack / siege); one
economy-literacy moment ("funds are the constraint, materials gate
construction, manpower gates armies, food gates growth"). Why: "a placed
building is not a working building" is the #1 new-player confusion, and
right now the answer is diagnosis-after-failure.

> **STATUS 2026-10-02 — SHIPPED.** Built on M1, not rebuilt: new
> `MissionDef.preplacedBuildings` (placed refunded/complete/unpowered
> in session setup) gives M1 one dark house by the player's base; new
> `onUnpoweredBuilding` / `onBuildingPowered` triggers + `requiresEvent`
> gating drive the warn-then-payoff beat ("a placed building is not a
> WORKING building until it is connected"); a new `m1-economy` atTick
> event teaches the four-resource mental model; skirmish sessions toast
> the right-click verbs (move / attack / siege) right after the B1
> objective toast.

### Tier 3 — Strategic identity and city depth

**D1. Doctrine asymmetry: Republic vs. Kestrel (M / Med / after
determinism hardening).** The design doc's C11 exists on paper —
implement it as doctrine overlays on the shared roster: Republic
(balanced, +sensors/+engineers, Aegis Battery signature) vs. Kestrel
Directorate (armor/artillery push, weaker AA, Tempest Cannon
signature), plus doctrine-gated National Program variants. Mostly data
and balance work; 1–2 signature units per side. Why: in Schell's terms,
the interesting choices are all tactical; the strategic choice of *who
to be* doesn't exist. This is the highest fun-per-effort *structural*
change — it multiplies every existing system (ages, counters, AI
personalities) instead of adding a new one. The AoE2 "positive
asymmetry" model keeps the full shared tech tree the design doc demands.

**→ SHIPPED 2026-10-02 (0.1 Alpha).** `sim/doctrine.ts` (new): per-owner
doctrines (AD9: snapshotted + digested, default 'republic'), stat
overlays via the existing `effective*` seams (Republic sight ×1.2,
Kestrel armor HP ×1.25 / damage ×1.15 / AA vsAir ×0.7, Republic engineer
cost ×0.8 / aura ×3 build + ×2 repair), two signature units
(`aegisBattery` Republic-only: intercepts Storm Engine strikes within
80u; `tempestCannon` Kestrel-only: 70-range siege gun with a 22u dead
zone), doctrine-gated program variants in `ages.ts` (Republic: Signals
Grid +12, Fiber Grid 1.35×, Green Tech 0.65×, Global Media ×1.125;
Kestrel: Heavy Industry 1.75×/1.35× upkeep, Cyber Command 1.75×,
Arsenal 1.4×/0.65× manpower), menu doctrine picker → session → AI
rival gets a seeded 50/50 doctrine. The AI plays its doctrine
automatically (same seams). 21 new tests in
`tests/sim.doctrine.test.ts`; roster 97→99 units.

**D2. Quantitative networks: give the city an optimization puzzle (M /
Med / after determinism hardening).** Make the existing networks
quantitative instead of boolean: power-line capacity, road-adjacency
throughput bonuses for factories/depots (roads become *useful*, not
mandatory — honoring the user's "roads optional" directive while giving
them a reason to exist), adjacency synergies between chain buildings
(refinery next to oil well). Why: Factorio's compulsion loop is "every
production step is a ratio puzzle *you* designed" — NOVATERRA has the
chains (quarry→factory→refinery→recycling is real) but no layout puzzle
around them. Systems tuning on infrastructure that already exists, not
new features. Explicitly *not* full traffic simulation — that's an L
with a real perf cost against the 60fps budget.

### Tier 4 — Signature moments (the three novel ideas, §4)

**E1. The Envoy at the Gates (S / Low-Med / after determinism
hardening).** Player-side only in 0.1 Alpha. Implement the combat
immunity gate and the Combine's never-target gate as **one shared flag**
(`neutralNonCombatant`), ideally in the same change. Coordinate the
`combat.ts` gate with whoever owns that file.

**E2. Luminaries (M / Med / after determinism hardening; no C1/C6/C7
conflict).** Ship 6 cards, not 24 — the deck is data, so this is a
content dial. Flag the writing quality bar explicitly: one great card
beats six flat ones. This is the drama engine the peaceful endless mode
needs — right now peaceful has no victory and no story beats; luminaries
give it a heartbeat without a single new combat unit.

**E3. The Vostok Combine, raid-less (M / Med / after determinism
hardening; coordinate `ai.ts` buy rule with the C1 owner).** Ship the
schedule + buy + AI-tell core; the freighter as a pure merchant. Add the
raid only after the targeting gate has proven itself via the Envoy. If
the team wants economic drama cheaper, a simpler lever — dynamic market
price shocks with Muse commentary — gets 70% of the fantasy at 20% of
the risk (documented as the fallback).

## 6. Dead ends (documented rejections — do not reopen without new evidence)

1. **Restoring full box-select.** The obvious P0 fix, deliberately *not*
   recommended. Left-drag is load-bearing for grab-pan; changing it means
   re-litigating the entire gesture contract (`camera.ts` "airtight
   rule," M1's explicit teaching). If ever revisited: modifier-based
   (Alt-drag = box-select) without touching the default — never a gesture
   swap.
2. **Full traffic simulation.** Rejected: real perf cost against the
   60fps-on-mid-range-laptop budget. The boolean→quantitative step (D2)
   gets 80% of the puzzle for 20% of the cost.
3. **The Combine raid in 0.1 Alpha.** The neutral-targeting gate is the
   biggest bug surface of the three ideas; North Star #1 is "no bugs."
   Ship raid-less (E3); the idea survives without it.
4. **Production-queue-free rally points.** Already cut in B4 ("no
   production queues exist, so rally would be dead UI") — only un-cut
   *with* real queues (C1).
5. **Reverting to world-global ages.** The old global age was a free-rider
   exploit; per-side ages are correct and rare. Never revisit.
6. **New units for novelty's sake.** The roster is 97; the civilian
   transport tab is the proof that more units ≠ more game. Roster
   discipline (C2d) comes before any new unit.
7. **Mayor auto-build as-is.** The mayor doesn't remove tedium, it
   removes the game. When the production model (C1) and city puzzle (D2)
   land, revisit the mayor's scope — automation must remove *chores*,
   not *decisions* (the Distant Worlds lesson from the game's own
   research, A6).

## 7. Sequencing vs. in-flight work (hard constraints)

- **Determinism hardening first for anything touching `sim/`.** B6, B7,
  B8, C1, C3, D1, D2, E1, E2, E3 all touch sim files — they queue behind
  the hardening pass, not beside it. The memory note stands: do NOT run
  the hardening concurrently with C1 (same sim files).
- **C3 multiplayer caveat.** The exploration-bets research found a
  Math.hypot cross-browser determinism caveat needing a 1–2 week fix
  before lockstep is real — the hardening pass is that fix. Fog of war
  (C3) additionally touches sight/simulation code paths; keep it off the
  hardening's critical path.
- **C1 (AI forward base).** B7's acceptance check depends on C1 landing;
  the Combine's `ai.ts` buy rule (E3) must be coordinated with the C1
  owner since both touch the AI think.
- **C6 (endscreens).** B3's end-of-game statistics should ship alongside
  the C6 art/drift — the screen gets prettier *and* earned in one pass.
- **C7 (visual day/night).** No conflicts with any item. (C7 explicitly
  rejected *gameplay* weather; none of these items touch weather.)
- **Campaign.** M7 is the campaign's slump (waiting game / grind quota)
  and the two endings are a scoreboard, not a consequence — but the
  campaign is out of scope for this plan's tiers; flag it as the next
  audit target after these items land. The C8 exploration-bet note
  (fiction consistency, Muse interludes, balance regression) stands.

## 8. What we explicitly don't touch

- The **logistics automation** (runMobileSupply, auto-loading depots) —
  the good kind: removes micro, keeps decisions.
- **Organic growth** — the city-builder engine that works.
- The **counter table and combat model** — minutes 12–20 are genuinely
  fun; don't fix what isn't broken.
- The **legible, non-cheating AI** foundation — the difficulty ladder is
  best practice; we add *scheduling* (B6) and *follow-through* (B7), not
  cheats.
- Anything in the C6/C7/determinism flight path — no concurrent sim edits.

## 9. Sources

Design literature and postmortems drawn on by the research team (see
`docs/research/game-design.md` Part A for the full genre research with
primary sources):

  first-60-seconds rule).

  escalating, telegraphed wave schedules as the heartbeat.

  *better* on a full shared tree).

  disclosed handicaps.
